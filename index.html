📚 Understanding Perplexity

Core concepts, probabilistic intuitions, and real-world evaluation insights.

🧩

What is a Token?

Language models do not process sentences character by character or word by word. Instead, they chop text into fragments called tokens using algorithms like Byte Pair Encoding (BPE).

Common words are often single tokens (e.g., "love"), while rare words are split into subwords. Spaces before words are also part of the token (represented visually by ␣).

Example: In DistilGPT-2, "I love Python" decomposes into 3 tokens: ["I", "␣love", "␣Python"].
🎯

What is Token Probability?

At each position, the neural network calculates raw unnormalized prediction scores (called logits) for every word in its vocabulary (~50,257 tokens).

The Softmax function turns these logits into valid probabilities ($0 < P \le 1$) that all sum up to exactly 1.0 (100%). It tells us: "Given all preceding words, how likely is this token?"

Numerically Stable Softmax: Subtracting $\max(z)$ prevents floating-point overflow:
P_i = exp(z_i - max(z)) / Σ exp(z_j - max(z))
🎲

What Does Perplexity Measure?

Perplexity (PPL) measures how surprised or confused the language model is by the text. Mathematically, it is the exponentiated cross-entropy loss or the geometric mean of reciprocal probabilities.

An intuitive way to think about perplexity is as the model's effective branching factor: a perplexity of 10 means the model is as uncertain as if it had to choose uniformly among 10 equally likely words at each step.

Dice Analogy: Rolling a fair 6-sided die has a perplexity of 6. A fair 8-sided die has a perplexity of 8 (as verified in Preset 2).
📉

Why Lower Perplexity Indicates Better Prediction

When evaluating models on the same text, lower perplexity indicates better prediction. A lower perplexity means the model assigned higher probabilities to the words that actually occurred in the sentence.

If a model predicts the exact next token with 100% confidence ($P = 1.0$) at every step, its perplexity is $1.0$ (perfect certainty, zero surprise).

Grammar Contrast: A fluent sentence like "I love Python" has much lower perplexity than scrambled words like "Python love I".
💥

Why a Surprising Word Spikes Perplexity

Because perplexity uses the geometric mean (or average negative log-likelihood), a single low-probability token severely penalizes the whole score!

If a token has probability $P = 0.01$ (1%), its reciprocal is $1 / P = 100$, and its surprise is $-\ln(0.01) \approx 4.61$. Even if every other token has 90% confidence, that single rare token dramatically elevates the average surprise.

See Preset 3: Three confident tokens ($P = 0.9$) plus one rare word ($P = 0.01$) increases perplexity from $\approx 1.11$ up to $\approx 3.42$!
